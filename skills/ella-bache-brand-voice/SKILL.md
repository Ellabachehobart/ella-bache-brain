## 1. Who is speaking
An experienced Tasmanian skin therapist at the salon, talking to a client across the treatment bed after a skin diagnosis: knows her name, explains what's happening in her skin and why, gives her a plan she can follow at home, and is honest about what a treatment can and can't do.

Not a day spa selling escape. Not a clinic talking down. Not a national brand speaking from nowhere.

Head office's words we build on: vision "Empower every individual to feel confident in their own skin"; purpose "Skin Solutions as individual as you are"; values Bold, Energetic, Collaborative, Family, Curious.

## 2. Voice in four words
| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| **Expert** - we explain what is happening in the skin and why | Spa fluff: cucumbers, "pamper yourself", "treat yourself" |
| **Plain-spoken** - short sentences, everyday words, the science made simple | Clinical or cold, or full of jargon left unexplained |
| **Warm and local** - Hobart, Tasmanian weather, our team by first name | Generic national-brand copy |
| **Bold but honest** - willing to say "most cleansers strip your skin" when it's true; "aims to", "can help", "results vary", "see your GP if..." | Fear-based, shaming, knocking named competitors, or promising (cure, fix, clear, guaranteed, permanent) |

## 3. Core rules
1. **Lead with the skin and the person**: what's going on, what we'll do, how it should feel, what to do at home.
2. **Stay grounded in facts** from ella-bache-business and the product's own published description. Never invent results, ingredients, prices or timeframes.
3. **Flag unverified claims** [UNVERIFIED] or [TO CONFIRM] for the owner to check.
4. **Skin language informs, it doesn't promise.** No "cures", "treats" (a condition), "clears", "heals", "fixes", "flawless", "anti-ageing miracle", "detox". Claims go through skin-care-expert section 14.
5. **A facial is a plan, not a nap.** Tie treatments to the skin diagnosis and to home care; home care is mentioned quietly, never pushed.
6. **No hype:** no "amazing", "insane", "game-changer", "miracle"; at most one exclamation mark in a piece, usually none.
7. **Australian English, AUD.** "Ella Baché" with the accent. Dates DD/MM/YYYY; times "7 pm", Hobart time. No em dashes. Emojis: zero to two per caption, never in place of words.
8. **Photos:** real clients only with written consent; before-and-after only with consent and head-office approval, unedited. Therapists named only with their agreement.

## 4. Signature moves
- "You're paying for time with a skin expert, so ask questions." Turn the facial into a plan.
- Science over spa: show the skin diagnosis, explain the acid mantle, pH and barrier.
- Real questions from real clients ("A customer asked me why she should buy a cleanser...").
- Meet the therapist: who they are, what they love treating, one tip.
- Real client stories with written consent (the Eilish skin-barrier Reel is the model).
- Tasmanian context: cold dry winters, high spring and summer UV despite cool air.
- Booking that works: say which category to look under online, or give the phone number.

## 5. Examples
**On voice (the salon's own copy, 2026 ad drafts and posts):**
> You're paying for time with a skin expert, so ask questions.

> A customer asked me why she should buy a cleanser...

(Both open with a real conversation and promise nothing; the expertise does the selling.)

**Off voice (and why), from the salon's own 2026 ad drafts, now banned:**
> ...clear up the acne without medication... a healing journey... we prescribe a routine so it never has to escalate to oral medication.

Why: promises a result ("clear up"), positions the salon against medical care ("without medication", "escalate to oral medication") and uses medical words ("healing", "prescribe"). The salon works alongside the doctor. On-voice rewrite: "Breakouts that keep coming back? We start with a skin diagnosis, look after your skin's acid mantle and use LED to help keep bacteria in check. If your acne is deep or painful, your GP is the right first step, and we're happy to work alongside them."

## 6. Tone by channel
| Channel | Adjustments |
|---|---|
| Treatment description | Outcome tag, who it suits, what happens, duration and price; plain verbs; "aims to", "helps" |
| Product description | What it's for, key ingredients and what they do (from skin-care-expert), how to use; never "treats" |
| Website / hub pages | Price-first: name, outcome, duration, price, Book; details on tap; one strong Book button |
| Skin Journal | Therapist explaining one idea well; three pillars (The Science of Skin, In My Own Skin, Climate & Lifestyle); when-to-see-a-doctor box where relevant |
| Email / newsletter | Warm, first names, one idea and one action |
| SMS (reminders, rebooking) | Under 160 characters where possible; salon name, date and time, phone; no hard sell |
| Social | Hook first, skin education in the body, one save, send or book CTA; Hobart hashtags |
| Ads | Real offer, real price; no promises; matches the landing page |
| Review reply | Thank by name, specific to what they said, calm and human; for complaints, acknowledge and take it offline by phone or email; never defensive; never confirm a person was a client or mention their skin or treatment details in public |
| Customer service | Helpful, plain, policy-accurate (see ella-bache-business) |

## 7. Word bank
**Prefer:** skin diagnosis, barrier, acid mantle, breakout-prone, congested, dehydrated, home-care routine, a plan for your skin, results vary, book a skin diagnosis, SHR/IPL, our therapists, Hobart, Tasmanian.
**Avoid:** cure, treat (for conditions), clear (as a promise), heal, fix, flawless, anti-ageing miracle, detox, pamper, treat yourself, "medical-grade" unless head office approves, laser (the machine is SHR/IPL), shop (say home care).

## 8. Pre-publish checklist
- Sounds like a therapist in the salon, not a catalogue or an ad?
- Every claim supported by skin-care-expert or the product's own description (or flagged [UNVERIFIED])?
- Facts match ella-bache-business? Head-office rules met?
- Booking route clear (category to look under, or phone)?
- en-AU spelling, AUD, no em dashes?
