# Content Marketing Writer

This skill is the **writing craft**: structure, hooks, headings, clarity and formats. It is brand-neutral.

## Works with (don't duplicate these here)
- **Tone and word choice** -> the brand voice skill (for Ella Baché: ella-bache-brand-voice). Brand voice overrides any style default below.
- **Facts about the business** -> ella-bache-business.
- **Facts about skin, treatments, ingredients, claims** -> skin-care-expert.
- **Which pages to write, update, merge or remove; target queries; priorities** -> the SEO skill (if the business has none yet, the owner decides, using Search Console data where available).
- **Product and collection copy at scale** -> the product content skill (if one exists).
- **Review requests and replies, GBP posts** -> ella-bache-local-seo.

## Principles
- **Write clearer, not more.** AI search doesn't reinvent SEO; it makes good content obvious and rewards clarity, freshness and context.
- **Quality = the reader can do what they came to do.** Not word count. People-first: original information, real expertise, enough for the reader to reach their goal.
- **Answer first.** Never bury the answer.
- **Every section stands alone** - humans skim and AI extracts chunks.

## 1. The brief (ask only what's missing)

- **Reader** - who, and what do they already know?
- **Job** - the one customer question or problem to resolve.
- **Primary query + 3-5 sub-questions** (from the SEO skill or search data where available; widen the list with the angles in section 2).
- **Action** - what the reader should do next.
- **Funnel stage** - Awareness (educate) -> Consideration (compare, prove) -> Decision (specifics, reassurance, offer). Match depth and CTA to the stage.
- **Proof and expertise** - stats, specs, examples, staff knowledge, original photos.
- **Frontline questions** - what customers ask staff that the content doesn't answer. Recurring questions are the best brief.
- **Channel and length.**
- **Target: SEO, AI or Both** (section 1a). Default Both.

If the request is clear, state assumptions in one line and write.

**Before writing a new page**, quickly check it passes three tests (full process in the SEO skill): a distinct need not covered elsewhere; something credible to add; a clear place on the site. If an existing page fits, recommend updating it instead. One keyword is not one page; no city-swapped duplicates; sharing keywords alone isn't a reason to merge.

## 1a. Target: SEO, AI or both

Two jobs, mostly the same craft. Pick the target in the brief (the SEO skill sets it in content plans) and say it in the assumptions line.

| | **SEO-led** | **AI-led** |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | Rank in Google and win the click | Be quoted, cited or named in AI answers (AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity) |
| **Use for** | Collections, product-adjacent guides, "best X", "[A] vs [B]", seasonal and brand + product or treatment queries | Questions people ask in full sentences: how to choose, what to expect, aftercare, "is X good for Y", treatment comparisons, salon/brand facts |
| **Title & meta** | Written for the click: keyword first, number or benefit, real reason to click (section 5, 8) | Clear and literal: says exactly what the page answers |
| **Keywords** | Exact primary keyword in title, H1, intro, URL; match the SERP's wording | Natural variants and customers' phrasing; full names of brands, models, materials, conditions |
| **Structure** | Template and length that beat what ranks (7.2); comparison tables; FAQ for snippets | Every H2 a question, first 1-2 sentences a complete answer that stands alone; fan-out coverage (section 2) |
| **Specifics** | Benefits and proof that make people choose us | Facts AI can lift: duration, price, key ingredients, aftercare steps, who it suits - stated plainly and consistently |
| **Repeat names** | Pronouns fine once context is set | Re-name the subject in each section ("the express facial", not "it") so each chunk makes sense alone |
| **Brand** | CTA to the collection or product | Name the business with the expertise in the answer itself ("our skin therapists find...") so the citation carries the brand |
| **Success** | Clicks, positions, CTR, sales (Search Console, GA4) | AI referral traffic, citations and mentions for the target questions |

**Both (default)** - body written AI-led (answer-first, self-contained, fan-out coverage, explicit facts); title, meta, URL and keyword placement written SEO-led. That's what most pages need.

**Never trade for either:** accuracy, answer-first, brand voice, or readability. If a tactic makes the page worse for a person, don't use it.

## 2. How AI search reads content

**Content -> AI understanding -> AI answer -> brand visibility** (mentioned, cited, summarised). If you don't connect the dots, AI fills the gaps and you lose the narrative. State the point and the reasoning explicitly in every section.

**Query fan-out:** AI search (Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, and others) splits one question into several related searches, then builds one answer. A page that answers the main question *and* the questions around it gets used more often than one that answers a single angle.

Find those sub-questions with these angles (skip any that don't fit):
- **Follow-up** - what they'll ask next ("how many facials before I see a difference?")
- **Broader** - the bigger context ("what makes a good skincare routine?")
- **Narrower** - a specific reader, use or constraint ("...for sensitive skin", "...for shift workers")
- **Implied** - requirements and consequences ("do I need to stop my retinol before a peel?")
- **Clarify** - where the query could mean two things ("peel" the salon treatment vs the at-home product)
- **Same question, other words** - the phrasing customers actually use; mirror it in headings

Cover, on the one page, the layers people ask together: **what it is -> why/when it matters -> how to choose or do it**. Lead with the page's main stage (section 1) and answer the others briefly.

Coverage is not padding: include only sub-questions this reader would plausibly ask. A sub-question with its own distinct intent (a collection, a product, another guide) gets a link, not a merged section - page decisions sit with the SEO skill.

## 3. PEE: Point, Evidence, Explain

- **Point** - a clear, direct statement in 1-2 sentences that makes sense lifted out on its own (no "as mentioned above").
- **Evidence** - stats, use cases, examples, specs, expertise, original photos, further context. Vague = uncitable.
- **Explain** - why it matters to this reader and what to do.

Apply to the whole piece, every H2 (first sentence = the point), and key sentences.

**Sentence test:** names who/what, the specific outcome, ideally the why. Replace "X is important / fast / reliable" with what X actually does.
- Before: "Good product descriptions are important for ecommerce."
- After: "Clear product descriptions help ecommerce brands increase conversions and reduce returns."
- Before: a page that only says "fast, reliable service". After: it states what's covered, where, availability, what happens, and how to book.

**Page test:** could the reader decide or buy without having to call and ask? If not, add the missing answers.

**Clarity, freshness, context:** one idea per paragraph; short sentences; define terms; specific names; current facts; who it's for and where it applies. An old date alone isn't a reason to rewrite an accurate page.

**Make relationships explicit:** name the thing, say what it does, to what ("Niacinamide helps strengthen the skin barrier, so skin holds water better", not "niacinamide matters"). Use one term per thing throughout (don't drift between "skin diagnosis", "skin analysis" and "consultation" unless you say how they differ). AI compares sources; vague or inconsistent pages get dropped.

**Claims you can stand behind:** every claim verifiable; explain *why* something works, not just that it does; no absolute rules where none exist ("usually", "for most people" when that's the truth). Cover related ideas instead of repeating the keyword.

## 4. Hooks (first 1-3 lines)

Types: **direct answer**, **specific number**, **pain mirror**, **contrarian**, **mini story** (2 sentences max), **question** (only the reader's exact question, answered next line).

Rules: specific over adjectives; promise only what's delivered; no clickbait or throat-clearing; social hooks must work in the first ~125 characters. Respect the brand voice (e.g. whatever the brand voice skill forbids).

Offer **3 hook options** of different types for new pieces; mark the recommended one.

## 5. Headings

- **Title/H1:** primary query + clear angle; primary keyword near the start; SEO title ~50-60 chars (too long or too short gets rewritten by Google).
- **H2s:** the reader's real questions or tasks, each answerable alone, answer in the first sentence.
- **H3s:** steps, options, sub-questions.
- The H2s alone should tell the story. Clear beats clever; personality goes in the copy.

Formulas: How to [result] without [pain]; [Number] [things] for [reader] ([year]); [A] vs [B]: which is better for [use]?; What is [term] (and why it matters for [reader]); [Result] in [timeframe]: [method]; **[Topic]: [actionable promise]**; **[Number] [specific adjective] [topic]**; **[Keyword question]? [Promise]**.

Be precise about the outcome (what they'll get, how long, for whom). A number usually lifts clicks. Use competitor titles as a starting point to beat, never to copy.

Offer **5 title options** when asked; recommend one.

## 6. Formats by channel

| Channel | Structure |
|---|---|
| Blog / guide | Validated topic + template (section 7) -> hook with answer -> question H2s with PEE -> comparison table if choosing -> FAQ -> next-step close + CTA; internal links to products/collections; promotion notes |
| Landing / collection page | Point above the fold -> proof -> how to choose -> FAQ -> one CTA |
| Email | Subject (specific, <50 chars) + preview text; one idea; one CTA |
| Social post | Hook line; 2-4 short lines; CTA or question; platform-appropriate length |
| Ad copy | Headline = need or product + benefit; description = proof + real offer; within platform limits; matches the landing page |
| Update brief | Exactly what to add/fix ("explain X, add Y, link Z"), not "refresh this page" |

**Repurposing:** offer a short social version (hook + 3 points + CTA) and an email subject line from longer pieces when useful.

## 7. Blog posts: engineer before you write

What you write about matters more than how. Settle topic, intent and template before drafting; then the writing is the easy half.

### 7.1 Validate the topic
Use at least one signal; say which in the assumptions line.
- **Customers and staff** - questions asked in the salon, by email or chat, before and just after buying; what reviewers use the product for and want more of. Strongest signal.
- **Communities** - Reddit, Facebook groups, forums where the reader hangs out. Look for repeated questions and threads with lots of replies.
- **Competitors** - their top pages by traffic, and content that still earns links and shares months later (evergreen), not one-day spikes. Data via the SEO skill.
- **Social test** - share the idea from a personal account first. Strong reactions, debate or questions = worth a full post.

### 7.2 Match intent, then pick a template
Look at the live results first: titles cited in the AI Overview, organic titles, video titles. Mostly "what is / how to" = informational; don't answer with a product list. If the template doesn't match intent, it won't rank for that query.

| Template | Use when | Example (illustrative only) |
|---|---|---|
| **List post** | Many discrete tips or options | 9 ways to look after your skin through a Tasmanian winter |
| **Case study** | A real customer or method with a result | How a nurse on 12-hour shifts built a skincare routine she sticks to |
| **Product showdown** | People search "[A] vs [B]" | [Treatment A] vs [Treatment B]: which suits dry skin? |
| **Tools of the trade** | "Best X for Y" | The products our therapists reach for after a facial |
| **Things to do after X** | A moment with a "now what?" | 7 things to do after your first facial |
| **Beginner's guide** | Newcomers to a narrow topic | The beginner's guide to salon facials |
| **Ultimate guide** | Broad topic people want in one place | The complete guide to caring for sensitive skin |
| **Myth debunker** | Common beliefs you can disprove with evidence | 7 skincare myths our therapists hear every week |
| **How they did it** | Learning from people who got a result | How 5 brides planned their skin before the wedding |

Beat what ranks: more items, or more useful detail per item than the current top results. Pick a core topic that's neither too broad nor too narrow: narrow enough to go deep ("growing tomatoes", not "gardening"), broad enough to answer the sub-questions around it (section 2). Claims about skin and conditions go through skin-care-expert.

### 7.2a Template blueprints
Every template still follows sections 3-5 and 7.3-7.6; these are the extra parts.

- **List post** - *Title:* number first + plain benefit (+ timeframe only if true). *Intro:* the reader's problem in a line, then the benefit. *Items:* subhead = what to do + specific benefit ("Book your facial a week before the event so skin can settle", not "Plan ahead"); best item first; each item has its steps and a photo or example. *Close:* push one action ("pick one to try this week").
- **Case study** - *Title:* specific result + a number + timeframe. Modest, relatable results beat dramatic ones. *Intro:* exactly what you'll show -> what makes the person relatable -> preview the process. *Meet the customer:* who they are, the problem in their words, one human detail, transition. *Results:* the outcome in numbers before the steps. *Steps:* every detail, with photos. *Close:* one-line recap -> "you can do this too" -> CTA. Real customer, consent and real numbers only; otherwise don't write it.
- **Product showdown** - *Title:* both names + category ("... two hydrating facials compared"). *Intro:* which products, the criteria you'll judge (who it suits, what happens, time, results to expect, aftercare, price), read to the end. *Overview:* each product briefly + why we're qualified (our therapists perform both; say we offer both). *Comparison:* one H2 per criterion as a question, most-wanted criterion first; a comparison table. *Close:* a clear verdict by reader type ("choose A if..., B if..."), not "it depends".
- **Things to do after X** - *Title:* the moment + number of steps. *Intro:* picture the moment -> "now what?" -> the options they're weighing -> promise the steps. *Each step:* imperative subhead -> short why (50-90 words, in customers' own words from reviews and questions) -> exact steps. *Close:* why the "after" matters -> the 2 most important steps -> CTA.
- **Beginner's guide** - *Title:* narrow topic + "beginner's guide". *Intro:* promise -> acknowledge it's confusing -> preview. *Overview:* plain definition + one example + transition. *Steps:* the first few steps only, each with a short why, exact how and a transition. *Close:* where to go next (next guide, book a skin diagnosis).
- **Myth debunker** - *Title:* "[Number] [topic] myths every [reader] should know" or one big myth + more. *Intro:* name the most common myth plainly, promise evidence not opinion. *Each myth:* where it came from -> evidence (data, treatment-room experience) -> why it's wrong -> what to do instead. *Close:* the most surprising myth -> CTA. Calm and factual; no outrage (brand voice).
- **How they did it** - *Title:* who + how many + what the reader learns. *Intro:* the opportunity -> why most miss it -> promise the method. *Each example:* proof of their result -> what they do differently -> why it works -> how the reader can apply it. Only use public, attributable information; never invent quotes.

Adapt from the source examples, don't copy their tone: no "insanely"/hype words, no exclamation marks, and no "leave a comment" CTA unless the blog has comments on - use book a skin diagnosis, call the salon or ask your therapist instead.

### 7.3 Intro
- Usually **5-12 sentences**; shorter when the reader already knows the topic. No long scene-setting.
- **PPP:** **Preview** (what they'll get) -> **Proof** (why trust us: years in the salon, staff training, results, a photo) -> **Preview** again (one specific thing coming) -> a **transition** line into the first section.
- Use the primary keyword once.
- Check: topic introduced; value obvious; reader knows what's coming; not over-explaining what they already know.

### 7.4 Body
- Paragraphs of **1-2 sentences** (most readers are on mobile).
- Descriptive subheads; a table of contents on long guides.
- **Active voice.** Read it aloud; rewrite anything that sounds stiff the way you'd say it (within the brand voice).
- **AI test:** could AI have written this section? If yes, it's an evidence problem, not a writing problem - add first-hand detail (what our therapists see in the treatment room, what worked and what didn't, real numbers). This is the Experience in E-E-A-T and what gets cited and linked.
- Visuals only when they explain or show something: original photos, skin diagnosis images (with consent), comparisons, how-to steps. Suggest them with a [ADD PHOTO: ...] placeholder.

### 7.5 Conclusion
A reader who gets this far is the most engaged one - don't waste it on a recap. A one-line recap is fine; a recap-only close isn't.
- **Specific heading** tied to a decision (not "Conclusion", "Final thoughts", "Wrapping up").
- **Make the next step obvious:** narrow 10 options to the top 2, or name the first thing to try.
- **Send them somewhere useful:** the related post, collection or booking page.
- Template-specific closes are in 7.2a (e.g. a showdown ends with a verdict by reader type).
- Don't: say "in conclusion"; write "now you know how to..."; drag it out; hold back anything important for the end.

### 7.6 On-page checklist
**SEO (always for SEO-led and Both):**
- URL: short, descriptive, natural words (about 4-7) close to the query.
- Exact primary keyword in the SEO title, H1 and once in the intro.
- Meta description per section 8.
- 2-5 internal links out to related posts and collections; name 2-3 older posts that should link to the new one.

**AI (always for AI-led and Both):**
- Each H2 is a question; its first 1-2 sentences answer it completely without the rest of the page.
- Fan-out sub-questions covered (section 2); FAQ for the leftovers.
- Subject re-named in each section; one term per thing; facts (sizes, widths, materials, care) stated plainly.
- The business and its expertise named inside answers, not only in the CTA.
- Last-updated date visible when facts can change (stock, prices, ranges).

### 7.7 Promotion notes (deliver with the draft)
- **Email:** plain text, little design; give real value in the email itself plus the link; send only the best pieces so opens stay high.
- **Social:** post from a person's account (owner or staff) - brand pages get far less reach. Repurpose per section 6.
- **Paid placement** (newsletters, podcasts, local communities the audience follows): only for the very best pieces.
- **AI search:** the structure above is also distribution; check later whether AI answers cite the page (the SEO skill).

## 8. CTAs, proof, links

- One primary CTA, after value is delivered; repeat at the end of long pieces. Make the next step obvious.
- Real proof only; missing proof -> [ADD STAT] / [ADD PROOF] placeholder, and tell the user.
- Suggest internal links to and from the piece.
- FAQ of real sub-questions, answered PEE-style. Tables for comparisons, lists for steps.
- Meta description ~140-155 chars that states the answer, includes the keyword and an action verb (learn, find, shop).

## 9. Rewriting existing content (the 3 fixes)

1. **Lead with an answer** - move it to the top of the page and each section.
2. **Support with proof** - add evidence next to the claims.
3. **Remove unnecessary complexity** - cut anything not adding to the answer; it's just buying words.
Flag stale facts and vague sentences, show before/after for the weakest 1-2 sections, and give a short prioritised fix list.

Final check: **Is it answering the question? Does it lead with facts and clarity? Could the reader act without asking?**

## 10. Output

Full piece: one-line assumptions (target SEO/AI/Both, topic signal, intent, template; note if an existing page should be updated instead) -> 3 hooks + 5 titles -> draft (H1/H2/H3, PEE, FAQ, next-step close, CTA) -> meta title + description + URL -> internal links -> placeholders -> promotion notes (blogs).
Short asks: deliver only what was asked.

## 11. Never
- Invent stats, quotes, studies, expertise or reviews.
- Filler intros, keyword stuffing, hype.
- Sections that only make sense in order.
- Padding for length.
- Recap-only conclusions.
